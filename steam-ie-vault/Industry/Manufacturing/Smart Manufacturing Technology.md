## What Is Smart Manufacturing?

## Overview

Smart manufacturing is a technology-driven production approach in which IIoT (Industrial Internet of Things) sensors, AI, and automation work together to create an intelligent, connected, and data-powered manufacturing environment. It integrates real-time data from machines, systems, and processes to improve decision-making, enhance efficiency, and enable seamless coordination across design, production, procurement, sales, and supply chain operations.

## Why are businesses shifting to smart manufacturing?

The global smart manufacturing market is projected to grow from its 2025 level of USD 394.35 billion to USD 998.99 billion by 2032 - according to a [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/smart-manufacturing-market-103594) study.  
  
The rise of emerging technologies in a demanding digitalized marketplace has compelled manufacturers to make smart manufacturing technologies an integral part of their growth strategy. On a fundamental level, this shift is marked by the following characteristics:

- The use of IoT (Internet of Things) devices to connect machines, people, and systems. Such interconnectivity ensures a seamless flow of at speed and scale.
- Enhancing accuracy and efficiency with automation - more so in repetitive tasks.
- Data-driven decision-making for the right actions at the right time to boost performance and minimize risks.
- Foster sustainability for optimal resource utilization and achieve sustainable goals.

## What are the core technologies that power smart manufacturing?

### Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT)

IIoT establishes an efficient interconnection of sensors, tools and machinery, tools and sensors that shake hands in the Cloud to share data, automate processes and extract insights to make swift and smart to enhance performance.

### Artificial intelligence (AI)

With AI, organizations are empowered to accomplish real-time data analysis based on the data collected (from equipment and assembly lines) by leveraging IoT tools. AI-powered algorithms can swiftly identify patterns, enabling manufacturers to continuously improve and adapt to changes, detect defects and anomalies, and correct them almost instantaneously. AI-driven smart manufacturing can also implement predictive maintenance and preempt safety hazards.

### Predictive analytics

This leverages advanced data analytics techniques to proactively identify inefficiencies and deterrents to efficiency, accuracy and quality - which, in turn, facilitates timely preemptive maintenance actions.

### Robotic process automation (RPA) & Industrial Robotics

From completing repetitive tasks with ease and accuracy to executing actions that are risky and dangerous, RPA is a critical aspect of industrial automation 4.0. Speed, versatility, accuracy and precision are RPA’s advantages - and when integrated with IIoT, analytics, it can create an extremely agile and efficient production environment.

### Cloud computing

The Cloud helps organizations to remotely manage data collection and storage, thereby enhancing data visibility in the supply chain. Cloud-based solutions help manufacturers to better leverage IIoT applications and edge computing to scale operations with ease.

### Blockchain

A shared ledger, Blockchain builds an almost perfect record of every step in the supply chain. It efficiently tracks goods movement to ensure transparency and utmost security to minimize frauds and improve accountability.  
  
Blockchain also improves the effectiveness of self-executing contracts. By directly writing the agreement into lines of code, one can verify product authenticity to track shipments and make payments.

### Digital twins

As a virtual replica of a system equipped with sensors and connected to the internet, digital twins collect data for real-time performance insights and anomaly detection. Scenarios can be simulated with digital twins before implementation of the right solutions.

### 3D printing

3D printing or additive manufacturing has revolutionized designing, prototyping and production of complex components with its speed and accuracy.

## Where can smart manufacturing solutions be used?

[Smart manufacturing solutions](https://www.mastek.com/industries/manufacturing/) can cover the gamut of maintenance, quality management, supply chain and manufacturing analytics.

### Maintenance

An integrated and intelligent cloud-based enterprise asset management system can integrate IoT, predictive analytics and automation design prescriptive workflows that are capable of preemptive action - both in manufacturing and supply chain operations.

### Quality management

IoT, AI and analytics-driven solutions give a connected and total view of product quality across product lifecycle and supply chain processes. Automation enables real-time price calculations and just-in-time order fulfillment.

### Supply chain planning

An end-to-end supply chain system in the cloud with AI and ML learning can seamlessly integrate demand insights, supply status and stakeholder inputs - to accelerate customer fulfilment and improve profitability

### Manufacturing analytics

Leveraging advanced analytics, AI, and ML, manufacturers can analyze real-time data from connected across equipment and supply chain management to optimize production, enhance quality and efficiency, and predict potential issues for proactive maintenance.

## What are the benefits of smart manufacturing?

- Flexibility in adapting to customer and market demands with speed and scale.
- High consistency in product quality.
- Minimized production downtime with analytics-led predictive maintenance.
- Improved worker safety.

## What are the challenges in smart manufacturing?



### High costs

Initial investments can be extremely high and can constrain medium and small enterprises from effectively competing with their larger counterparts. Starting with pilot projects to validate ROI, and leveraging cloud platforms can optimize costs.



### Limitations of legacy systems and infrastructure

With many manufacturing firms operating with outdated equipment and siloed systems, it is a challenge to integrate them with technologies such as IoT, AI, cloud, blockchain and more.  
  
Compatibility barriers can be overcome using middleware and IoT gateways to bridge legacy systems and modern technologies. 



### Vulnerability to cyberattacks

The interconnected system of machines runs the risk of data breaches and cyberattacks - not to mention complex compliance regulations across different regions. Manufacturers must ensure stringent security and compliance measures are in place.



### Skill gaps

The industry faces a shortage of data scientists, IoT engineers, and automation specialists. This skills gap is further compounded by worker resistance driven by concerns about job loss and organizational change.  
Talent gaps will need to be addressed by innovative learning and training programs. This will also effectively mitigate worker resistance due to apprehensions of job displacement.



### Cybersecurity vulnerabilities

Vulnerable endpoints, legacy protocols, and inadequately designed security policies for operational technologies increase the surface for cyberattacks on interconnected systems.

Manufacturers must guard themselves against security risks through zero-trust architecture, effective network segmentation, and regular risk assessments and penetration testing,
